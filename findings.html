What I've found so far

Early results from five states and 367 counties. These are preliminary.

Help follows people more than need

When I split each state's counties in half by need, the higher-need half had 59% more overdose deaths per person but only 7% more harm reduction sites. These are the middle counties in each half.

Splitting by population instead tells a different story: the bigger half of counties has 93% of the people and 88% of the sites. Where people are, sites mostly follow.

State law matters, but it doesn't explain the gaps on its own

All five states allow syringe programs, and none allows overdose prevention centers. Indiana still makes it a crime for a person living with HIV to share needles. But every county in a state has the same law, and help still varies a lot from county to county. Two Indiana counties of the same size, LaPorte and Delaware, are 84 and 16 miles from the nearest syringe program.
